SLC Transcript Review Guide

For checkers of the SLC meeting transcript. Reading time: 5 minutes. Review time: about 30–45 minutes per piece.

In one line: open your review_yourname.html file in Chrome, Edge or Firefox; listen to each line; fix the words and the speaker name; tap ✓ Confirm; then tap Download my edits and send that file back on WhatsApp.

1. What you received

  • review_yourname.html: your personal review page. The meeting audio for your pieces is inside this one file (about 5–7 MB). No internet, login or app install needed.
  • This guide.

Each piece is about 4 minutes of the meeting. You usually have 5 pieces (about 20 minutes of audio), in order, so the conversation carries on from one piece to the next.

2. Open your page

Use a laptop or desktop if you can. Typing and listening is much easier with a keyboard and headphones.

Laptop / desktop (recommended)

  1. On WhatsApp Web or desktop, download the .html file.
  2. Find it in your Downloads folder.
  3. Right-click → Open with → Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

Android phone

  1. In WhatsApp, tap the file → it downloads.
  2. Open Chrome → ⋮ menu → Downloads → tap the file.
  3. If it shows code or a blank page, use a laptop instead.

iPhone: not supported (iPhones can't run these pages from a file). Please use a laptop.

The first time you open it, a short help box appears. You can reopen it anytime with the Help button.

3. Review, line by line

  1. Pick a piece from the tabs at the top (Piece 1 · 0/27 means 0 of 27 lines done).
  2. Tap ▶ Play on a line. It plays just that line. Use Speed to slow down to 0.75×, or tick keep playing to hear what follows.
  3. Fix the words in the box so they match what was said, word for word.
  4. Fix the speaker (see section 4).
  5. Tap ✓ Confirm when the line is correct. It turns green. Only confirm lines you have actually listened to.

Laptop shortcuts (while typing in a line): Ctrl+Space plays that line again; Ctrl+Enter confirms it and jumps to the next line and plays it.

Writing rules

DoDon't
Write exactly what was said, in the speaker's words, including half sentences and repeats that change the meaning.Don't summarise, tidy up grammar, or "improve" what someone said.
Write Hindi/Tamil words in English letters as heard, e.g. "haan, theek hai".Don't translate.
Numbers as digits: 30 balls, 6,500 rupees, 49 lakh, 1.4 crore.Don't guess numbers: if unsure, use [unclear].
Use correct names: Gymkhana, Schroeter, Inter IIT, Saathi, E-Cell, CFI, GSB.Don't leave obvious mis-hearings like "Jimkhana".
Put [unclear] where words can't be made out, and add a short note.Don't invent words to fill a gap.
Leave out pure fillers ("umm", "uh") unless they matter.Don't delete real words.

Wrong line break?

The computer sometimes splits one person's sentence across two lines, or puts the start of the next speaker's words at the end of a line. Move the words to the line where they belong (cut from one box, paste into the other). If a whole sentence is missing, add it to the nearest line and write "added missing sentence" in the note.

4. Speaker names

The computer can't name people yet, so lines show Unknown A, Unknown B… Within one piece the same letter is usually the same voice, but not always: always check.

  • Type the name the same way the meeting notes do. Suggestions appear as you type.
  • When you change a speaker, the page offers to set the same name on the other unconfirmed lines with that letter in the piece. Say Yes only if you're confident, then still listen to each line.
  • If you don't know who it is, leave it as Unknown and confirm the words anyway. That still helps.

5. Orange lines: people talking over each other

Lines with an orange edge and the tag several voices overlap with another line. Listen carefully: make sure each person's words are on their own line, and not repeated on both. If two people truly speak at once and you can't separate them, write what you can and add the note "overlap".

The grey tag speaker unsure just means the computer guessed the speaker. Check the name like any other line.

6. Save and send your work

  1. Tap Download my edits (top right). A small file named like edits_yourname_20261020-2130.json is saved to your Downloads.
  2. Send that file to the SLC Secretariat on WhatsApp: attach it as a Document.
  3. Do this every time you stop, even halfway. Always send the newest file. Older files are safe to send too; they never overwrite newer work.
  • Your work is also kept inside the browser, so you can close the page and continue later on the same device and browser. The top bar shows Not sent yet when you have changes you haven't downloaded.
  • Changing device? Open your page on the new device, tap Help → Load an edits file, and pick your latest edits file. You continue where you left off.
  • Private/incognito windows forget your work when closed. Don't use them, or download often.

8. Please keep it confidential

The page contains a recording of an SLC meeting. Don't forward the file or share the audio. Delete the .html file once you've sent your final edits.

Questions: contact the SLC Secretariat. Thank you for helping make an accurate record of the meeting.
